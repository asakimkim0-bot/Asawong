There is a particular kind of internet heartbreak that only creators understand: watching an account you spent so much time building disappear. For the creator behind @yunieranter, that loss became an unexpected reset.

The account's previous chapter had reportedly reached more than 23.5 million likes and 23.6 thousand followers before it was lost and deleted. Those numbers are part of the creator's own story; the old profile is no longer available to visitors, so its history cannot be independently checked here.

A Killua-inspired corner of TikTok

The new account keeps a clear visual identity: dark blue, white, and Killua, the sharp-witted character from Hunter × Hunter. The look gives the page a recognizable mood while the creator focuses on rant-style videos, fandom opinions, and the kind of quick takes that invite people to stop scrolling and join the conversation.

Rather than presenting the new page as an overnight comeback, the story is still unfolding. @yunieranter is growing again, one post and one new viewer at a time. A fresh account means rebuilding an audience from the beginning, even when the person behind it has already experienced a much bigger platform.

"A lost account can take the numbers away. It doesn't take away the ideas—or the person making them."

Young creator, firm boundaries

The creator is 14 and says the new account was started in Seoul, South Korea. She also comes from a wealthy family, but chooses not to identify her family or share private background details publicly. That boundary is intentional: she wants the content to be the focus, not her private life.

Keeping personal details offline can be a sensible choice, especially for a young creator. @yunieranter's public-facing identity is the username, the Killua-inspired design, and the videos she chooses to share—not her home address, school, relatives, or private life.

The next chapter

For now, the new account is a work in progress. There are no promises of instant virality, and no need to turn every personal detail into content. The goal is simpler: keep creating, find the right audience, and let the page develop naturally.

For creators who have had to start over, that may be the most relatable part of the story. The old numbers are history; the next post is still theirs to make.
